# indigenous journalism

Journalism by indigenous journalists

- Educate people on Indigenous history, cultures and perspectives
- Counters erasure and symbolic annihilation by framing indigenous people as persistent and in relationship with nature
- Emphasizes resilience and resurgence
- Draws on Indigenous knowledge and expertise
- Criticizes the push for objectivity and distancing in mainstream media
	- <= hides the influence of cultural norms
	- <= doesn't consider Indigenous voices
- Uses social media

## Indigenous people in North America

- Canada:
	- First Nations, Inuit and Métis
	- 1.8 million people
	- 70+ different languages
- US:
	- Native Americans, Alaska Natives, Pacific Islanders
	- 4-7 million people
	- 169 different languages

## Settler colonialism

- Indigenous people alienated for nationalism
- Occupation of land disrupts Indigenous relationships to land => violence

## Mainstream journalism

- Only reports on indigenous communities when something bad happens
- Cultural appropriation
- Low trust by indigenous communities
- Canada:
	- Surge in public interest in recent years
	- Deficit model: representation of Indigenous people as degenerate and unable to manage their own affairs
		- Rooted in settler colonialism
- US:
	- Symbolic annihilation: omission or trivialization of certain groups by mass media
		- Invisibility as new form of racism
